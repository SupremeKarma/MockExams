Q: Why is the conductivity of an electrolyte very low compared to a metal at room temperature?
A) Electrolytes have more free electrons than metals
B) Charge in an electrolyte is carried by ions, which are much heavier and slower-moving than the free electrons that carry charge in a metal
C) Electrolytes are always solid at room temperature
D) Metals have no free charge carriers at all
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: In a metal, conduction is due to highly mobile free electrons, while in an electrolyte, conduction relies on the drift of much heavier and slower ions through the solution — this drastically lower ionic mobility gives electrolytes far lower conductivity than metals.

Q: What is a key advantage of A.C. (alternating current) over D.C. (direct current) for power transmission?

Q: In what sense is the production of X-rays the "inverse phenomenon" of the photoelectric effect?
A) X-ray production converts light energy into electrons, same as the photoelectric effect
B) In the photoelectric effect, photons striking a metal eject electrons; in X-ray production, fast electrons striking a metal target are suddenly decelerated, converting their kinetic energy into photons (X-rays)
C) X-rays and the photoelectric effect are unrelated phenomena
D) X-ray production only occurs at very low electron energies
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: The photoelectric effect converts photon energy into electron kinetic energy (light → electrons); X-ray production (via bremsstrahlung) is the reverse process — high-speed electrons decelerating rapidly in a target convert their kinetic energy into photon (X-ray) energy, hence "inverse."

Q: Is polarization possible for longitudinal waves, such as sound waves?
A) Yes, all wave types can be polarized equally
B) No — polarization requires the wave's oscillation to be transverse (perpendicular to propagation), so purely longitudinal waves like sound cannot be polarized
C) Only if the wave travels through a vacuum
D) Polarization only applies to electromagnetic waves, never mechanical waves
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: Polarization refers to restricting the oscillation direction of a transverse wave to a single plane. Since longitudinal waves (like sound) oscillate along the same direction as propagation rather than perpendicular to it, there is no transverse oscillation to restrict — so longitudinal waves cannot be polarized.

Q: Why does an ammeter have a very low resistance?
A) So it draws maximum current for accurate readings
B) So that inserting it in series with a circuit disturbs the original current in that branch as little as possible
C) High resistance would make it more accurate
D) Ammeters are required by law to have zero resistance
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: An ammeter is connected in series to measure current, so it must have very low resistance to avoid significantly altering the total circuit resistance and thus the actual current being measured — a low-resistance ammeter gives a reading close to the true, undisturbed current.

Q: How is a galvanometer converted into an ammeter?
A) By connecting a high resistance in series with it
B) By connecting a low resistance (a "shunt") in parallel with the galvanometer, diverting most of the current around it
C) By removing its coil entirely
D) By connecting it directly to a battery with no other components
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: A galvanometer is converted into an ammeter by connecting a low-value shunt resistance in parallel with it — this diverts most of the current through the shunt, allowing only a small, safe fraction to pass through the sensitive galvanometer coil, extending its range to measure larger currents.

Q: What is the Doppler effect?
A) A change in the amplitude of a wave over long distances
B) The apparent change in the observed frequency of a wave due to relative motion between the source and the observer
C) A phenomenon that only occurs with light waves
D) The complete absorption of sound in a vacuum
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: The Doppler effect describes how the observed frequency of a wave (sound, light, etc.) shifts higher when the source and observer move closer together, and shifts lower when they move apart, due to their relative motion.

Q: In the He-Ne (Helium-Neon) laser, what is the primary role of the helium atoms?
A) Helium emits the laser light directly
B) Helium atoms are excited by electrical discharge and transfer their energy to neon atoms via collisions, helping achieve population inversion in neon, which then emits the laser light
C) Helium is used only to cool the laser tube
D) Helium absorbs the laser output to control its intensity
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: In a He-Ne laser, an electrical discharge excites helium atoms first; these excited helium atoms then collide with neon atoms and transfer energy to them efficiently, achieving the population inversion in neon's energy levels needed for stimulated emission — the actual laser light output comes from the neon transitions.

Q: What did Michelson's method measure, and what was its general principle?
A) It measured electrical resistance using a rotating coil
B) It measured the velocity of light using a rotating mirror (or octagonal mirror) setup, timing how long light took to travel a known distance and return
C) It measured the mass of the electron using a magnetic field
D) It measured atmospheric pressure using a mercury column
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: Michelson's method for measuring the velocity of light used a rotating mirror (in later refinements, an octagonal rotating mirror) reflecting light over a precisely known distance to a fixed mirror and back; by finding the rotation speed needed for the returning light to align with the next mirror facet, the time of flight — and hence the speed of light — could be calculated.
