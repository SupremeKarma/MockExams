Q: What is the defining characteristic of "fundamental research" (also called basic/pure research)?
A) It solves a specific, immediate business problem
B) It seeks to expand general knowledge/theory without a specific practical application in mind
C) It is always conducted using secondary data only
D) It cannot be published in academic journals
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: Fundamental (pure/basic) research aims to expand theoretical understanding and general scientific knowledge, rather than solve an immediate, applied practical problem — that is the domain of applied research.

Q: What distinguishes "action research" from other project research approaches?
A) It focuses purely on historical data analysis
B) It is conducted to solve an immediate, practical problem within a specific organizational context, often through iterative cycles of action and reflection
C) It never involves real-world implementation
D) It excludes qualitative data entirely
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: Action research is applied, participatory research aimed at solving an immediate practical problem within a specific context, typically through repeated cycles of planning, acting, observing, and reflecting.

Q: What is the key difference between primary data and secondary data in research?
A) Primary data is collected firsthand by the researcher for the specific study; secondary data is pre-existing data collected by someone else for another purpose
B) Primary data is always numeric; secondary data is always textual
C) Secondary data is more accurate than primary data
D) There is no meaningful difference
ANSWER: A
EXPLAIN: Primary data is original data gathered directly by the researcher (e.g. surveys, experiments) for the current study, while secondary data is existing data originally collected for a different purpose that the researcher reuses.

Q: In sampling, what best describes "snowball sampling"?
A) A form of probability sampling using random number tables
B) A non-probability technique where existing study subjects recruit future subjects from among their acquaintances
C) Selecting every 10th person from a list
D) Sampling only from a single fixed location
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: Snowball sampling is a non-probability method where initial participants refer or recruit additional participants from their social network, useful for reaching hard-to-access populations.

Q: In hypothesis testing, what does the "level of significance" represent?
A) The sample size required for the study
B) The probability threshold for rejecting a true null hypothesis (Type I error rate), commonly denoted alpha
C) The correlation coefficient between two variables
D) The number of independent variables in the study
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: The level of significance (alpha) is the probability threshold (e.g. 5% or 1%) at which the researcher is willing to risk incorrectly rejecting a true null hypothesis (a Type I error).
DIFFICULTY: medium
MARKS: 5

A) Digital reports and printed reports
B) Technical reports and popular reports
C) Long reports and short reports only
D) Verbal reports and written reports only
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: Research reports are broadly classified as technical reports (detailed, methodology-focused, for a specialist audience) and popular reports (simplified, results-focused, for a general audience).

Q: What is the main purpose of a bibliography section in a research report?
A) To summarize the researcher's conclusions
B) To list all sources, references, and works cited or consulted during the research, supporting academic credibility and traceability
C) To present the raw survey data
D) To describe the sampling method used
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: A bibliography lists every source consulted or cited in the research, giving readers a way to verify claims, trace prior work, and supporting the academic integrity of the report.
