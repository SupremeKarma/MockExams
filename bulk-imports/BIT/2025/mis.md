Q: In an organizational hierarchy, which level of information system is typically used to support day-to-day operational decisions?
A) Executive Information Systems (EIS), used only by top management
B) Transaction Processing Systems (TPS), used at the operational level to record routine daily business transactions
C) Strategic planning systems used exclusively by the board of directors
D) None — all information systems serve only senior management
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: Organizations typically have different information systems for each hierarchy level — Transaction Processing Systems (TPS) support operational-level staff handling routine daily transactions, while systems like MIS, DSS, and EIS serve middle and senior management for higher-level decision-making.

Q: How do information systems typically impact organizations, according to MIS theory?
A) They have no measurable effect on organizational structure or processes
B) They can flatten organizational hierarchies, change reporting relationships, and redefine business processes and job roles
C) They only affect the IT department, not the rest of the organization
D) They eliminate the need for any organizational structure
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: Information systems significantly reshape organizations — they can flatten hierarchies by enabling wider spans of control, change how decisions are made and by whom, redesign business processes, and alter job roles and required skills across the organization.

Q: What is "IT infrastructure" composed of?
A) Only the company's internet connection
B) The shared technology resources — hardware, software, networks, facilities, and services — that provide the foundation for an organization's specific information systems
C) Only the software applications used by employees

Q: What is a "business process" in the context of management information systems?
A) A single isolated task performed by one employee
B) A logically related set of activities that define how specific business tasks are performed to accomplish organizational goals, often spanning multiple functional areas
C) Only the accounting department's month-end closing routine
D) A synonym for "software application"
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: A business process is a set of logically related activities and tasks — such as order fulfillment or product development — that an organization performs to achieve a specific business goal, often coordinating work across functional areas like sales, manufacturing, and finance.

Q: What is the primary purpose of the "value chain model" in analyzing a business?
A) To calculate employee salaries
B) To identify the specific primary and support activities in a firm where competitive advantage and value can be added
C) To design a company's office layout
D) To track only marketing expenditures
ANSWER: B
EXPLAIN: The value chain model, developed by Michael Porter, breaks a firm's activities into primary activities (e.g. inbound logistics, operations, sales) and support activities (e.g. HR, technology, procurement), helping identify exactly where in the chain competitive advantage or value can be created.

Q: What does "vulnerability" mean in the context of information systems security?
A) A system that has never been attacked
B) A weakness or exposure point in a system that could be exploited by a threat, potentially compromising confidentiality, integrity, or availability of data
C) A synonym for "firewall"
D) Only applicable to hardware, never software

EXPLAIN: Vulnerability refers to a weakness or gap in an information system's security controls — such as unpatched software, weak passwords, or misconfigured networks — that a threat actor could exploit to compromise the system's confidentiality, integrity, or availability.
